# Cyber Security

## Research and Learn

### What are common cyber security threats in a remote work environment?

 - Phising and social engineering
 - Unsecured (public and private) networks
 - Bring Your Own Device
    - Devices from home that may not have up-to-date antivirus, full-disk encryption, or even the existence of an antivirus
 - Weak/Reused credentials
    - Reusing passwords
    - Not using more secure methods (password manager or MFA)
 - Unapproved applications
    - Installing and using unauthorized cloud storage, file-sharing apps, or collaboration tools bypassing IT compliance and safety controls
 - Malware/ransomware
 - Physical theft or loss
    - losing unencrypted devices in a public space

### What are best practices for keeping your devices and accounts secure?

 - Use unique login credentials for each account
    - utilizing passphrases with multiple characters, numbers, and varying capitalization
 - Use multifactor authentication
 - Use a password manager
 - Audit and remove inactive or previously compromised accounts
 - Ensure software is up-to-date
 - Ensure screen locks after inactivity
 - Require biometrics in favor of traditional passwords
 - Install antivirus/antimalware software
 - Limit application permissions
 - Enable tracking and remote wiping
 - Avoid public WiFi
 - Avoid public USB ports
 - Verify all links, attachments, and email addresses
 - Regularly back up all sensitive data in a secure location (physical or highly-secure remote)

### Why is it important to lock your computer when away from your desk?

This prevents unauthorized access to sensitive data and materials existing on the device.

### How should you handle phishing attempts and suspicious links?

Do not click on any of the links, respond to the email or open/download any attachments. Get a screenshot/evidence and send it through to the cybersecurity team so they are aware of this event/method, then delete the message completely.

### What makes a strong password, and why should you use a password manager?

What makes a strong password?
    - Must be relatively long (recommended 16 characters)
    - Complexity/randomness
        - Mix of characters, numbers, and case
        - Mix of unrelated words (for a passphrase)
    - Uniqueness
        - Unique password for each account
    - NO PERSONAL INFORMATION
    - AVOID REPEATED CHARACTERS

Why should you use a password manager?
    - Stops credential stuffing
        - prevents all of your accounts being exposed during a data breach
    - Eliminates human error
        - removes the habit of using/reusing simple passwords, writing passwords down on sticky notes/in text files or saving sensitive login credentials to a web browser
    - Automates logins safely
    - Monitors breaches for you, do not need to wait for the announcement from the company/organization that experienced the breach
    - Stores more than just passwords
        - Securely holds a variety of sensitive information, such as IDs, passkeys, private notes, etc.

### Why is two-factor authentication (2FA) important, and when should you enable it?

MFA is important as it blocks most automated and password-related attacks (some of the most common), by simply requiring a second form of identification. It should always be enabled for any account or service that supports it.

### How can you make secure behaviour a habit rather than an afterthought?

1. Remove inconveniences (make it easy)
2. Practice habit stacking (tie the new habit to a pre-existing one so it better integrates)
3. Start small (create microhabits)
4. Create environmental reminders (physical items to remind you, such as a note on your desk)
5. Shift the mindset - make it something tied to your identity rather than a task that you have to complete
    - Ex. "I am using a password manager because I want to ensure my passwords and accounts are fully protected"
