### What are examples of professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment? (Hint: Think about communication, meeting etiquette, and teamwork.)

Professional:
 - Communication
    - Using clear, polite language in chats, emails, and media
    - Proactively updating teammates when timelines change
    - Giving constructive feedback directly
 - Etiquette
    - Arriving on time (or messaging ahead if delayed)
    - Arriving with a goal or point to be raised already in mind
    - Letting everyone share their thoughs
 - Teamwork
    - Taking ownership of mistakes/completed work (accountability)
    - Sharing credit for group wins
    - Holding open, constructive, and respectful discussions/debates with teammates

Unprofessional:
 - Communication
    - Using overly-familiar or demeaning nicknames
    - Gossiping in group channels
    - Venting complaints/personal drama constantly
 - Etiquette
    - Monopolizing the conversation
    - Multitasking non-essential tasks (in a distracting way to yourself and others)
    - Constantly letting meetings run past their scheduled end time
    - Not arriving on time for meetings
 - Teamwork
    - Passing the blame for mistakes
    - Not carrying your own weight
    - Treating junior staff with less respect

### What does respectful communication look like in remote teams? (e.g., tone in messages, handling disagreements, responding to feedback)

- Tone
    - Lead with context, state 'why' before the 'what' so it is not an abrupt demand
    - Utilize softening cues, use emojis, clear punctuation, or a brief greeting to replace missing voice inflection and body language
    - Match urgency to channels, reserve fast-paced pings for real emergencies - use async docs or threads for routine updates
    - Avoid midnight pings, respect boundaries and time zones
 - Handling Disagreements
    - Move to video/voice, shift overly heated or complex conversations to a video/voice medium as this allows for tone and empathy to be shown
    - Focus on the behavior not the people, describe specific actions do not attack the person's character
    - Assume positive intent, give peers the benefit of the doubt do not assume malice
    - Document resolutions, note down agreements after a debate so there exists a single source of truth for future reference
 - Responding to Feedback
    - Pause before reacting, take time to digest constructive criticism as opposed to sending an immediate, defensive reply
    - Ask clarifying questions, treat feedback as data to solve a shared problem
    - Acknowledge and close the loop, validate the person's perspective, state the adjustment you plan to make, and follow up later to show progress

### How can you give and receive feedback professionally? (e.g., focus on actions, not people; assume good intent; ask for clarification instead of reacting defensively)

- Focus on Actions and Impact, not People
    - Address behavior not character, describe what happened instead of labeling the person
    - Quantify the impact, explain how the action affected the team or the project
    - Use 'I' Statements, frame observations from your perspective to avoid sounding accusatory
- Assume good intent
    - As previously mentioned, assume that teammates and coworkers have good intentions and do not give feedback with spiteful/malicious intentions
- Ask for clarification, do not act defensively
    - As seen in the previous question:
        - Pause before reacting, take time to digest constructive criticism as opposed to sending an immediate, defensive reply
        - Ask clarifying questions, treat feedback as data to solve a shared problem
        - Acknowledge and close the loop, validate the person's perspective, state the adjustment you plan to make, and follow up later to show progress

## Reflection

### Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?

Yes I have, I have experienced it through my casual work. For us, the thing that ensures we uphold the standard of great teamwork and professionalism is the quality of our communication - we ensure that everyone is aware of what is happening at all times so everyone is able to step in and help or react to any changes. We also ensure that it is a comfortable and friendly environment so no one feels afraid to ask for assistance if the task requires more than one person or if there have been unexpected blockers.

### What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

For me to ensure that my communication remains clear, respectful and inclusive the steps I would take is first know who my audience is. After this, I would ensure that I retain a constant level of politeness and respect within my messages/responses, as well as retaining a honesty and directness. In terms of inclusiveness, I would also make sure that I am aware of my audience's situation and my own to ensure that I am understanding and compassionate without making anyone feel uncomfortable.

### How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

- Come in with a positive attitude - greet each day with a smile
- Treat all colleagues with respect
- Support and facilitate open communication
- Trust others in their work and their intentions
- Be supportive, whether it is giving recognition for someone's achievement or just helping someone with a small blocker

### How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

 - Come with questions/updates already in mind
 - Preemptively read the brief for the meeting
 - Ensure that all software/documents to be presented (if needed to be presented) are ready to be accessed and used prior to meeting start

### What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

 - Conduct research first/give it a college try
    - Ensure that I exhaust all of my own options when hit with a problem (Google, OpenAI, colleagues) before approaching my supervisor
 - Come to every meeting prepared
    - Ensure that I have all my questions, updates, and ideas prepared prior to entering the meeting
 - External research
    - Do my own research outside of working hours to upskill/better prepare for tasks
 - Communicate Early
    - Ensure that I identify and flag potential blockers long before their deadline to prevent waiting until something goes wrong

### How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information/action from them? How would you escalate it further if you don't get a response and when would you escalate?

For me, I would first determine the urgency of the request - as this will determine the level of communication required (for example, a small problem or question could be posed through an email or using discord if slightly more urgent - a phone call or direct text message is reserved for pure emergencies). I would then pose my question in a polite, respectful way while noting all the important details of the situation so that the colleague/supervisor are completely aware and informed of my thinking and the problem at hand. In terms of escalation, I would start from the lowest level first and give the acceptable grace period for the form of communication (for example, email is 48 hours, discord is 24, etc.). If I do not receive a response by the accepted grace period, I then would move up the chain of command with my question as fitting with the urgency of the request - if it is a low-level request then I would ask another colleague or move up the chain just one spot, if it is a highly urgent request or question, then I would escalate to the supervisor.

### What are some things you won't do?

 - Not carry my weight - I will ensure that I complete the work that is allocated to me and not push it off to another colleague or 'coast'
 - Be disrespectful in any capacity - verbal, written, or otherwise
 - Harass my supervisor with questions, only seek them out after all other options are exhausted (or the situation is dire enough)
 - Not communicate progress/leave colleagues in the dark
 - Not attend meetings
 - Not come to meetings unprepared
