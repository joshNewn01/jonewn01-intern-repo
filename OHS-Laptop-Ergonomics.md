# Occupational Health & Safety (OHS) for Desk-Based Work

## Research & Learn

### What are the risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard?

When looking at the risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard, there are a few key areas to watch out for. For no external monitor, the risks are largely postural and eye-focused - such as the lack of a larger screen can result in eye strain or the screen/keyboard being connected creates a difficult position for viewing the screen and utilizing the keys. For no external keyboard, this largely results in muscle strains and postural issues - such as constraining hand and finger positioning/more errors due to a smaller keyboard size and more closely-spaced keys or an awkward forward position of the keys encouraging the resting of wrists/hands on the area between the keys and the user (can lead to Repetitive Strain Injury(RSI), such as Carpal Tunnel Syndrome).

### What ergonomic equipment can improve posture when working on a laptop?

For improving posture and avoiding injury during work on a laptop the following equipment can be utilized:

 - External Monitor
 - External Keyboard
 - Keyboard riser
 - Ergonomic Desk Chair
 - Ergonomic Chair Cushion
 - Footrests

### What adjustments should be made to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup for a healthier workspace?

 Monitors:
     - Top of monitor screen should be 10 degrees below your eyes
     - Monitor should be a comfortable viewing distance away to prevent unnecessary head movements (about an arm's length away)
     - If two screens are used, then the main screen should (must) be directly in front of the user with the secondary monitor directly next to it

Chairs:
     - Chair height must be to best fit your work station - feet must be flat on the floor
     - Knees must be around level with hips
     - If armrests are available, position them so your arms sit gently on the armrests with elbows close to the body and shoulders relaxed/not tensed
     - Chair must be centered on the desk/in line with main monitor
     - Ensure chair back is supporting your back

Desks:
     - Invest in a sit/stand desk for varying posture (one position for a long time is bad for us)
     - Don't store items under your desk (restricts room for legs/feet)
     - If desk has hard/sharp edges, pad the edges or use a wrist rest

### What are some daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use?

Eye Strain:
 - Follow the 20-20-20 Rule
    - Every 20 minutes, look at something approximately 20 feet (6 meters) away for at least 20 seconds
    - Allows focusing system to relax
 - Blink more often
    - Maintains healthy tear film and supports eye comfort
 - Take regular breaks

 Sitting Posture/Movement:
  - Routine scheduled movement, shifting sitting position every 30 minutes
  - Utilize earphones/remote communicative devices to allow walk and talk
  - When taking a food break, eat a healthy lunch away from your desk
  - Vary the work tasks so that posture is changed and different parts of the body are utilized
  - Find more active ways to get to work and getting around in general:
    - Combine a meeting with a walk
    - Park a bit further away
    - Get off one bus stop earlier
    - Use the stairs instead of the elevator
    - Go for a walk during lunch break or to locate lunch
