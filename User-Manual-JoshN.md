# Creating Your "User Manual for Me"

## Research and Learn

### What is a "User Manual for Me". and why do teams use them?
 - What?
   - A User Manual for Me is a short, self-authored document detailing the needs/habits an individual requires to perform their best - typically including communication style, core values, pet peeves, feedback preferences, etc.
 - Why?
   - Faster onboarding
   - Fewer misunderstandings
   - Better collaboration and communication
   - Psychological safety

### What categories are helpful to include?

As previously mentioned, some of the better categories to have are:
 - Communication style/habits
 - Core values
 - What keeps you focused/what breaks focus
 - Pet peeves (in teamwork and in general)
 - Feedback preferences
 - Any additional information that would be good to notify team members and make the experience easier for all parties involved

### How can writing a personal user manual improve teamwork and reduce misunderstandings?

Writing a personal user manual improves teamwork by making initially implicity work styles, boundaries, needs, and communication preferences explicit, removing guesswork in how an individual prefers to be worked with and reduces unnecessary internal friction.

## Reflection

### What do you need from teammates to do your best work? (e.g., direct communication, flexibility, detailed instructions, minimal interruptions)

For me, I prefer a few things from teammates:
 - Direct communication - always has been a key factor for past success
 - Detailed instructions - I like to ensure that I am producing work exactly as planned, and detailed instructions help with this

### How do you prefer to communicate? (e.g., async messages, video calls, quick check-ins, long discussions)

For me, my preferred method of communication is whichever one gets the idea across the best while allowing for nuance and personability to be presented. Though I am okay with written discussions, I much prefer verbal communication as it allows for the finer details to be discussed a little easier - so quick verbal check ins, long discussions, and video calls work best for me, but I am capable of written communication should it be needed.

### What helps you stay focused and productive? (e.g., quiet workspace, deep work sessions, structured to-do lists, music)

For me, the most pivotal is a quiet workspace - I can get very easily distracted by external noise or commotion which throws off my train of thought. I love deep work sessions where I am able to quietly grind away at my tasks. Occasionally I will listen to music while I work, but it depends on the work - if it is a more mindless task then it is nice to have, but if the task is very thought-intensive then I tend to avoid it.

### How do you like to give and receive feedback? (e.g., clear & direct, with positive reinforcement, written vs. verbal feedback)

I am incredibly flexible with feedback, as I am always looking to learn and improve. In terms of giving feedback I appreciate it when people are direct and clear with me, so I make sure to be as well. When I give feedback, I always try to incorporate some sort of positive reinforcement or positive angle as it allows for more proactive engagement with the feedback as well as showing I appreciate the work done and want team morale as high as possible. For the type of feedback, I am completely okay with both verbal and written, but I do appreciate written feedback as it gives me something to look back on and reference.

### What are some quirks or things to know about your work style? (e.g., need time to process ideas, like to brainstorm out loud, prefer written instructions)

Frankly most of my quirks are the ones that are listed as examples for this question/thought point. I do need time to process ideas, as I want to ensure that when I present them, they are worded in a way that exactly explains my thoughts and reasoning as well as being as fleshed out as possible (this can be seen in meetings or conversations when I will go quiet trying to formulate a response). I do like to brainstorm out loud, typically through a quiet conversation to myself, but I also will wander/pace as it helps my thinking. Written instructions are very much preferred, as I do find myself struggling to fully hear or digest everything that is told to me when it is presented entirely verbally.
